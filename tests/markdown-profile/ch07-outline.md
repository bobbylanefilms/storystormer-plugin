## Scene 1: The Ferry Dock
pov: Ada Vale
summary: Ada boards the last ferry and finds her seat taken by the man from the harbor office.

Ada reaches the dock as the gangway lifts and talks her way aboard with the ticket she bought for yesterday. The cabin is nearly empty, but her seat by the window is taken by the man she saw arguing with the harbormaster that morning. He does not move. She sits across from him and counts what he carries:

- a canvas satchel, buckled twice
- a paper sack of oranges he never opens
- a ferry timetable with tonight’s crossing circled in pencil

When the horn sounds he looks at her for the first time and asks whether she is the one from the paper.

## Scene 2: The Manifest
kind: found_document
summary: The cargo manifest Ada photographs, listing three crates with no consignee.

The manifest is a carbon copy on onionskin, pinned inside the purser’s window. It lists the night’s freight by crate number, weight and consignee, in the purser’s block capitals. Three crates near the bottom carry a weight and no name.

* * *

A second hand has added a note in the margin: “Hold for collection at the far landing. Do not open.” The note is dated tomorrow.

## Scene 3: The Far Landing
pov: Ada Vale
summary: Ada learns the truth: the crates were never cargo, and the man with the oranges came to collect them.
authorship: author_written

Fog takes the far landing before the ferry docks. Ada watches the deckhands carry the three crates down first, ahead of the mail, and set them on the stones apart from everything else. The man with the oranges is the only passenger waiting for them. He signs for them with a name that is not the one on his ticket.

## Scene 4

Ada follows him as far as the end of the pier and loses him at the fish market. She keeps the photograph of the manifest and the ticket stub, and she writes the false name on the back of both.

## Synopsis

Ada talks her way onto the last ferry and is seated across from a man she saw arguing with the harbormaster. A cargo manifest in the purser’s window lists three crates with no consignee and a margin note, dated the next day, holding them for collection. At the far landing the crates come off first, the man signs for them under a false name, and Ada loses him at the fish market with a photograph of the manifest and his assumed name in hand. She now knows the crossing was arranged for the crates, and the man knows who she is.
