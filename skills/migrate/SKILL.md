# StoryStormer · Migrate

You are moving a story from the store it lives in now (the **source**) to another backend the author chooses (the **target**). Everything goes through `references/store.md`: the source is read through its binding, the target is written through its binding, and nothing here names a backend’s tools. The source is never modified, and this skill never offers to delete it; the author deletes it by hand once they are satisfied, and the report says where it is.

The skill’s other mode is the **layout upgrade** (§ The layout upgrade): an old-layout project (`references/file-schemas.md` § The old layout) converted to layout 2 in place, in the store it already lives in, with no move. Its conversion (§ Converting the old layout) is also what a move from an old-layout source runs on the way to its target (§ From an old-layout source).

Always follow `references/plan-first.md`. The **preflight is the plan**: nothing is created on the target, and nothing in an upgrade is written, until the author has seen it and said go.

## What you produce

- The project on the target: every document the target can hold, written through its binding in canonical order, in layout 2, with a config of `layout` and the target’s own `storage` block and nothing else (`file-schemas.md` § `.storystormer/config.json`).
- The **flip**: the one write outside the target, the open folder’s `.storystormer/config.json` `storage` block, so the next session resolves to the new home (§ Verify, then flip).
- A report: counts by category, anything stored as unmapped on the target, anything skipped and why, where the untouched source is, and how to go back.
- **In the layout upgrade** instead: the same project converted to layout 2 in its own store, with no move and no flip (§ The layout upgrade).

## Source and target

**Source** is the session’s binding, resolved per `store.md` § Resolve. In a hybrid session (a Dropbox project bound locally through `local_root`), the source is that Dropbox project, read through the local binding, which is the fast path to the same files.

**Target** options are init’s (`storystormer-init` § Where a new project lives): this folder, when there is an open folder; and each backend in `store.md` § Bindings that is not pending and whose binding’s tool check passes. Leave out the source’s own kind (both `local` and `dropbox` in a hybrid session, since they are the same files). With one option left, name it and confirm; with none, say why each backend is unavailable (pending, connector missing, it is the source, no open folder) and stop. After a completed move the session resolves to the new home, so the old kind is an ordinary target again (a move back: § Same slug recognizes the copy the earlier move left there and offers a plain flip when it still matches, else the refusal and merge); a completed move is never re-copied to the home it already reached.

**The slug** is the source’s: the Dropbox folder name, the Notion row’s `Slug`, or for a local project the folder’s name in kebab case, NFC (the title’s kebab case when the folder name won’t serve). The preflight names it; the author can change it. Validate it, whoever chose it (a Notion source’s `Slug` included): kebab case, `[a-z0-9-]` only, NFC, no `/`, no `..`, no leading dot. Anything else, ask for a slug that passes rather than repair it silently. **A changed slug travels:** when the author changes it at preflight, the copy writes the new `slug` in the root `state.md` and the new `storystormer_project` in every entry, and the plan names both changes (with the entry count); without a change, both stay as the source has them.

**A local target** is the open folder. It may hold nothing but the pointer config that sends the session to the source (and OS clutter); anything else is named, and the author is asked before anything is written into it. A folder holding a full config is an existing project (§ Same slug), and another slug can’t help, since the destination is the folder itself: offer to merge into it, or to open or choose another folder and run `migrate` there.

## The preflight (the plan)

`store.list('**')` on the source, then work out, without writing anything:

- **Counts by category:** canon (`characters/`, `worldbuilding/`), story documents (`primer.md`, `treatment.md`, `manifest.md`, `series.md`, `voice/`, `research/`), `outline/`, chapters (folders and artifacts), the collections (decision, question and journal **entries**, counted from the listing of `decisions/`, `questions/` and `journal/`; from an old-layout source, counted from the `## D-###` and `## Q-###` headings of `decisions.md` and `questions.md`, a log the source doesn’t have counting as zero entries, not an error, § From an old-layout source), snapshots (`.storystormer/history/` folders and each chapter’s `.history/`), the init archive (§ What happens to each item), and everything else. A series counts the shared root, then each book. The plan shows every category with its count, zero included (“init archive: none found”), so an empty category is visible to the author rather than silently absent.
- **The inventory, cross-checked.** Check the listing against an independent count where the source binding offers one, and for completeness where it offers only that (its `list` realization says which): on Notion, the root row’s relation lists are a second count for its rows, while its child pages are only checked for completeness (the row’s fetch whole, every tag mapped); on Dropbox, `has_more` paged to its end, and on a folder the listing’s count check, are completeness signals, not a second count. A shortfall is listed again, never planned around: the copy and the verification both work from this inventory, so a gap here would balance on both sides and pass unseen.
- **What normalization will change**, when the target’s binding normalizes author markup on the way in (its § Rendering): read each markdown document and count, by file and by construct, what `references/markdown-profile.md` § Rewrite table will rewrite (count non-breaking spaces by byte, U+00A0, never by eye: a read shows them as spaces, and a live run’s normalizers reported none in two files holding 36) (`primer.md`: 1 HTML table → pipe table; `chapters/chapter-03/ch03-prose.md`: 14 two-space line breaks → bare newlines), plus, listed apart, any construct the profile puts under No in-profile form, which the target changes and nothing keeps, and every bare filename or path whose entity the target decodes (`The_Good_Dog_-_Brainstorming_&amp;_Future_Plot_Points.md` → `…_&_…`: the profile’s § Kept as written), since after the move the text no longer names the real file. Documents already in the profile change nothing and are counted as such. Past `references/subagent-pattern.md` § When to dispatch’s threshold, counting subagents do these reads, one per category, read-only, with the rewrite table and the two lists pasted as the copy briefs carry them, and return counts by file and construct only, never document text, so the main session’s window holds the plan, not the project. Approving the plan approves these rewrites; the source is never changed.
- **From an old-layout source,** the conversion’s plan items as well (§ From an old-layout source).
- **Mixed layouts.** A source with no `layout` that holds both a log and its collection (`decisions.md` beside `decisions/`, or the questions pair) is an interrupted layout upgrade: stop, and tell the author to finish the upgrade first (`/storystormer:migrate upgrade`), since neither half alone is the project. A layout-2 source whose root still holds `decisions.md` or `questions.md` (left by an upgrade on a host that couldn’t delete them) names them in the plan as skipped: they are never copied, not even as unmapped documents, since the upgrade’s snapshot holds them and layout 2 never reads them.
- **What Exists text that won’t survive.** Every target recomputes `state.md`’s What Exists (the table below), so read that section in each source `state.md` and sort what is in it. **Computed lines:** the first `- **<Label>** — …` line for each label `file-schemas.md` § `state.md` lists (or the series form’s lines), one line per label; the target recomputes them, so say once that their own prose is replaced by the recomputed form. **Carried lines:** a `- **<Label>** — …` line whose label names documents the project holds but isn’t a computed label (`**Research**`, `**Handoff**`, `**Methodology**`); the target keeps it as written (`file-schemas.md` § `state.md`, update rules; on Notion, the binding’s carried lines). **Everything else is dropped:** a second line with a label already seen (a repeated `- **Prose chapters** —`), a sub-bullet under a line, a free paragraph, a retained earlier count line. Name each dropped piece by its first few words and its word count, and say the source keeps it. With none, say so.
- **Computed documents with the author’s own text.** Where the target computes `characters/_index.md`, `outline/_index.md` or `voice/README.md` (the table below), the source’s copy is replaced by the target’s rendering. Compare each source copy with what the target will compute from the same documents, and name any text the rendering won’t hold (an index’s hand-written notes, a tier or one-liner column, a “Not yet written” list) by its first few words and its word count; say the source keeps it. With none, say so.
- **Damaged rows** (a Notion source): any row whose `Meta` the binding reports as damaged (`references/store/notion.md` § Frontmatter ↔ properties, rows stored in the earlier form) is named with its note: its frontmatter crosses as the typed properties render it, and its source frontmatter can’t be recovered from Notion.
- **What happens to each**, by the table below: copied, mapped to the target’s realization, computed by the target, stored as unmapped, or skipped (with the reason and the paths).
- **Same slug** on the target (§ Same slug): clear, or the refusal and its merge prompt, or a recognized unfinished or flip-pending earlier run.
- **Names the target can’t keep apart.** On Dropbox, whose paths are case-insensitive and must be NFC (`references/store/dropbox.md` § Quirks), list any source paths that differ only by case and any names not in NFC; on a local target on a case-insensitive filesystem (macOS’s default), list the paths that differ only by case (NFC is Dropbox’s rule, not checked here). Either way, stop for the author’s call (rename in the source, or skip one) rather than collide mid-run.
- **Where it will live** (the Dropbox path, the Notion hub and whether a new hub and tables are created, or this folder) and **what the flip will write** into the open folder, or that there is no open folder to flip.
- **Call volume**, estimated from the counts and the target’s capabilities line, roughly: on a local target, one write per document; on Dropbox (`write: delete-create`), one create per document plus one per new folder, and about four calls and 20 seconds for each document an overwrite replaces (delete, one poll, create, and a verify read, as observed); on Notion, three to four per document (create, the async poll, a second content call when the body opens with an H1 (`# `), and the read-back, § Copy), the decision, question and journal entries in batches (one create carries several rows; a live run sent 68 decisions in 10 creates) with their numbering checks, plus about ten for a new hub. Add the source’s reads: one per document, two on Notion, and into Notion the preflight’s reads for the normalization count. A 150-document project with 200 log entries is on the order of five hundred calls into Notion and a couple of hundred into Dropbox; a live run of 97 documents and 109 entries took about 310 Notion calls and over an hour. Into Notion, the config’s last write is usually a no-op: its fields are the Projects row’s properties, set at scaffolding, so it writes only what changed since. Moving back to a copy left behind normalizes that copy’s documents to compare them (§ Same slug), model work that takes minutes on a project of a hundred documents; count it in the estimate. Say plainly that a big project takes a while, that an interrupted run resumes by running `migrate` again (identical writes are no-ops), and that edits made elsewhere during the move land only in the source.

Then wait for the author’s go. That approval is the confirmation for every mutation in the run (`store.md` § Confirmation posture), on every binding.

**A copy keeps IDs.** Every number the target reports for an entry (`store.md` § New entries) must equal the source’s: an entry reported under another number (a race, which a new project shouldn’t meet) is a `mismatch` that blocks the flip, reported with both numbers and never accepted, since the source’s chapters and entries cite the old one.

**From a store that computes documents** (Notion → a folder or Dropbox), each rendered read is written as a stored file: each entry as its rendered document, the indexes and `voice/README.md` as rendered, child pages as files at their path titles, `.storystormer/intake/**` as ordinary files (an init archive among them re-filed per the table). The rendered config is written with its `storage` block replaced by the target’s. An old-layout Notion source renders `decisions.md` and `questions.md` as logs (its binding’s § Old-layout projects), which the conversion splits like any other (§ From an old-layout source). **A second rendering of every frontmatter.** Nothing on the target checks a rendering, so each document’s frontmatter is rendered twice, by the copy subagent from its read and, independently, by another agent (or the main session): a row’s from a rows query of the same rows (properties and `Meta` only: about one query per hundred rows), a child page’s from a second fetch of that page (its frontmatter is the `yaml` fence at the top of its body, `references/store/notion.md` § Frontmatter ↔ properties, Child pages). The checking agent compares its rendering with the file as written (or its read-back), and the two must match exactly, the one empty line after the frontmatter included when the document has a body; any difference is a `mismatch` on that document (*observed (2026-10-24)*: without it, a quoting slip on `act: 1` in four chapter files and a lost empty line after a style guide’s frontmatter crossed unseen). A document that entered Notion through its normalization comes back in profile form, exactly as it was sent; text an author wrote in Notion, or that reached it before that normalization, comes back in Notion’s form (`references/store/notion.md` § Rendering, Known limit), and the report says so once.

## Same slug

Use the target binding’s own § Locate and § Scaffolding checks: on Dropbox, a `/StoryStormer/<slug>` folder holding a config (the raw listing, compared case-insensitively); on Notion, a Projects row with that `Slug` in the chosen hub; on a local target, a full config in the open folder.

- **Clear:** go on. On Notion, after the go, run the binding’s § Scaffolding steps 1–2 only as needed (a hub and tables when there is no hub) and step 3 always (the Projects row, and every book row in a series), created from the frontmatter and config fields with no page content; Dropbox runs its steps 1–2 (slug check, folders).
- **Taken:** refuse, say what is there (its title, stage and last session from that project’s `state.md`), and ask whether the author meant to **merge** into it or to pick another slug. Don’t guess.
- **An unfinished earlier run of this migration:** the target holds the slug but no config (a stored target) or a Projects row with no content (Notion), every document it holds is the target path of a source path under this skill’s own mapping (the table above, the init archive’s re-filing included: a Notion target holds `.storystormer/intake/<timestamp>/…` for a local `.storystormer/history/<timestamp>-init/…`, and the other way round), and each matches the source’s exactly (the target binding’s rendered read against the source document, in its normalized form when the target normalizes: § Copy, the normalized form) or is one of the per-document cases named there (a construct with no in-profile form; a document that differs since, such as one an interrupted run left mismatched or half-written). Those are listed and shown to the author, both versions, and the resume rewrites them. A target `state.md` (written just before the config, so present when a run stopped between them) matches when it differs from the source’s only by the migration’s own transforms: What Exists and the Storage line (from an old-layout source, the conversion’s `state.md`, checked as below). From an old-layout source, a converted entry or journal entry matches when it keeps every word of what it came from with its fields right (§ The layout upgrade, Resume), not by exact comparison, since two conversions of one entry may differ harmlessly. Say so and offer to **resume**: a merge into that folder or row with nothing to conflict, where every identical write is a no-op. A configless folder or content-less row that doesn’t fit this (it holds a path the source doesn’t, or the author doesn’t recognize the differences shown) is someone else’s; ask before writing into it, as the binding does.
- **Complete, flip pending:** the same, but the target’s config and `state.md` were written (the run stopped between them and the flip). It is not taken: say so and offer to finish, which re-runs verification (§ Verify, then flip) and does the flip.
- **The copy an earlier move left behind** (a move back to an earlier home): the target holds the slug with its own full config, the two hold the same documents, and every one matches, compared exactly as above: the two rendered reads, each in its normalized form when either side’s binding normalizes (normalizing text already in the profile changes nothing, so in effect it is the copy that never went through Notion that gets normalized, and a flip back to it returns its original markup, which the report says). Leave out only what the table above skips or computes and the differences a migration makes by design: the config’s `storage` block, What Exists, and the Storage line. Everything else in the config and every `state.md` is compared too (a source and a copy in different layouts never match: one has the logs, the other the collections), so a later change the author made there (`target_word_count`, `current_focus`, What’s Next) is never discarded. When all of that matches, it is not taken: say so and offer to **just flip**, with no copy. The flip writes the open folder’s config `storage` block as the target’s, whether it names the source or the target now (a pointer to another project is left alone, § Verify, then flip): when the target is this folder (the original local project, whose config still names the backend it moved to), its `storage` becomes `{"kind": "local"}`; otherwise the pointer becomes the target’s block (§ Verify, then flip). Then the session rebinds there. Any document that differs (in the compared parts) or exists on one side only means it is not a plain left-behind copy: show the differences and ask, with the refusal and merge prompt as usual.

**Merge** means: write the source over the target path by path through the target binding, with its concurrency guard (the baseline is the target’s read at preflight) and, where its `history` is `snapshots`, a snapshot of each document replaced (operation `migrate`, per `store.md`). Never delete a target-only document. The collections merge by entry: a source entry the target lacks is added under its own number (a journal entry, at its own path); one whose number or path the target holds with different content is a **conflict**, not copied, and listed. Anything that references a held-back entry is held back too and listed beside it (a question whose `resolved_by` names it, a `superseded_by` naming it, a journal entry or chapter or other document citing it), so nothing attaches to the target’s different entry under that number. A stored target’s indexes then get a row per added entry (`file-schemas.md` § Who rebuilds an index). A guard mismatch is a conflict too: stop that document, show the author both versions, write nothing until they choose. The report lists every conflict and every document replaced.

## Copy

**Order.** The canonical order (canon; story documents; outline; chapters; the collections; everything else; snapshots) has only these real barriers: **canon first**, alone, because a target that indexes cross-references needs it in place (Notion links each chapter row to the characters and places it names); **decisions before questions** inside the collections category (a `resolved_by` links a row); **chapters in chapter order** inside the chapters category; and in a series, **shared root first, then each book in `books` order** inside every category. After canon the categories don’t depend on each other, so they run in parallel. `state.md` and then the config come last, in that order, from the main session, after verification (§ Verify, then flip): the config is the very last write to the target, so a half-finished stored target has no config and is recognizable as unfinished.

**The normalized form.** On a target whose binding normalizes author markup on the way in, each document is written in its **normalized form**: the source’s text with `references/markdown-profile.md` § Rewrite table applied, and nothing else. The table is a markup-only mapping, idempotent by design: it leaves words, code, frontmatter and everything under § Kept as written alone, and text already in the profile (anything the plugin wrote, and anything read back from a Notion row written with this normalization) normalizes to itself. Applying it is model work, since there is no script at runtime, so the table is what makes it repeatable: a resume in a fresh session recomputes the normalized form from the source and must get the same text. **A miss made consistently would pass unseen,** since the read-back compares against the same normalized text, so before writing, a different agent from the one that normalized the document (another subagent, or the main session) goes through the rewrite table row by row over the normalized text, looking at lines rather than reading for sense (for the block rows: the first line of every heading, table, quote, list and fence has an empty line or the document’s edge above it and its last line has one below it, and a field label line under the last item of a list that sits under a field label has one above it; for the emphasis rows: every `*`, `_` and `~` run is checked against the table’s forms): it must find nothing, and anything it finds is a rewrite the first pass missed, which is applied and counted against the preflight’s numbers (*observed (2026-10-24)*: the normalizer’s own second pass missed a whole row, nine headings run into paragraphs in one file; in the second ring a different agent reading for sense missed eighteen in two files). Right after each write the document is read back through the target binding and compared with that form exactly. Every comparison this skill makes against such a target is exact against it, and is a comparison of renderings, never of what a row stores (a log row’s `Meta`, where its `Resolution` starts, the order of its `_as_written` entries or a derived `Name` are the writer’s choices, and two correct writes may store them differently), and where exact can’t hold, the case is handled by name, never by a looser match:

- **A construct with no in-profile form** (the profile’s § No in-profile form): the target changes it and nothing keeps it, so the plan names the document, its read-back `mismatch` is reported with the difference rather than blocking, and a resume or left-behind check shows that document to the author instead of counting it as matched.
- **A mismatch that a rewrite reproduces:** when a document failed its read-back and one rewrite (a retry or a resume) gives the same mismatch, rewriting again can’t help and would keep the flip blocked for good. Stop rewriting it, show the author both versions and the difference, and ask: name it for this run, where it then counts as a No in-profile form case (reported with its difference, not blocking the flip, and listed as such in the report), or stop the run there, leaving the source as it is and the target unflipped.
- **A document edited in Notion since the move,** or a recomputed normalization that comes out different: it doesn’t match, and is treated as any differing document is (both versions shown, the author chooses between rewriting it and the refusal and merge prompt).
- **A row stored before this normalization** (a 0.20.x `init` or `migrate`), or one whose `Meta` the binding reports as damaged (`references/store/notion.md` § Frontmatter ↔ properties, rows stored in the earlier form; its note travels with the document into the report): it can read back in Notion’s own form, which the table still changes (split emphasis such as `**bold *****inner***** bold**`). So on such a project, moving back to a copy left behind shows those documents as differing even when no one edited them: the author sees each with both versions and the difference, and chooses as for any differing document.
- **An entry** is a document like any other: each is read back and compared exactly with its normalized form (a converted entry, with the converted text as written, § From an old-layout source). A computed index is read once after its collection lands, and must list every ID once.

**Subagents.** Past `references/subagent-pattern.md` § When to dispatch’s threshold, dispatch one execution subagent per category: canon first and alone, and wait for its report before dispatching the rest (a host may launch subagents asynchronously whatever synchronous option the call offers, as observed in Cowork), then the others in a single message so they run in parallel, each keeping the order its own category needs (above). Below it, copy directly in the main session. Every brief carries two store blocks, as `storystormer-init`’s intake briefs do: a **source** block (binding, root, capabilities line, its `read` and `list` realizations; read-only) and a **target** block (binding, root, capabilities line, the realization of every operation used, and the triggered clauses pasted as text: write recovery where `write` is not `native`, the confirmation posture, the documents the target computes and must not be written except through the realizations named for them, and on a merge or resume each target’s baseline and snapshot path), **plus every binding section those realizations cite, pasted as text**, since a subagent can’t open the bundle. On Notion that is the hub’s five data-source URLs, the Projects row id (and book row ids in a series), the path map, frontmatter ↔ properties (with `file-schemas.md`’s frontmatter key order for each kind of document the brief writes, which `_keys` is measured against), rendering, § IDs (with the import rule), § Async (with its two-in-a-row rule: once two consecutive calls fail to queue, the rest go synchronously) and query pagination, and the run’s async state, since the count belongs to the run: the brief carries the run’s count of consecutive queueing failures in, and when the run has already fallen back to synchronous sends it says so and the subagent sends synchronously from its first call; the collections subagent also gets the entry realizations, § IDs with its import rule, and `file-schemas.md` § Entry collections’ formats verbatim, and from an old-layout source the conversion as § Converting the old layout’s briefs carry it. A target that normalizes also needs `references/markdown-profile.md` § Rewrite table, § Kept as written and § No in-profile form, which normalization applies. On Dropbox it is § Write, its recovery and the quirks the writes depend on (case, NFC, the trailing newline). The rule for any binding: whatever a pasted realization refers to travels with it. Each brief lists its paths with source path → target path and the action (copy, or skip and why), and asks for a per-path log: written, identical (no-op), stored as unmapped, skipped (only a skip the approved plan named, with its reason), conflict, **mismatch** (the read-back differs from the normalized form, with the first line that differs), or **failed** (an error the binding’s recovery couldn’t clear, with the error), with counts, plus `async: switched` when the subagent fell back to synchronous sends (the binding’s § Async): the main session then briefs every later dispatch to send synchronously from its first call and reports the switch once. A path the plan didn’t skip is never logged as skipped. Reject a log that names a local filesystem path for a non-local target (`subagent-pattern.md` § Mandatory brief fields) and re-dispatch.

The main session aggregates the per-path logs. A renumbered entry is a `mismatch` (§ What happens to each item, a copy keeps IDs), reported with both IDs.

## Verify, then flip

**Verify.** Before writing the target’s `state.md` and config, `store.list('**')` on the target and check, leaving out the written-last documents (every `state.md` and the config, which don’t exist yet on a fresh target):

- **Sizes, into a folder:** when the target is a folder and the source’s listing gives each file’s size in bytes (Dropbox), each written file’s size must equal the listed one, allowing for the final newline: a read ends every document in exactly one `\n` (`references/store.md` § Operations, the final newline), so a source file with no final newline is written one byte larger, and one ending in several `\n` smaller by the extra ones. The source binding’s read says how many the stored file had (the Dropbox binding’s read does), and the copy subagent logs that count with the path; any other difference, wherever the host can measure a written file, is a `mismatch`. It costs no extra call, and it is the only content check a copy into a folder gets. Where the host can’t measure, say so.
- **Paths:** every path the logs call written, identical, mismatch or stored as unmapped appears in the listing. Checking the path set, not just a count, also catches a merge where pre-existing target files would mask a missing one.
- **Balance, per category:** the source’s count equals written + identical + mismatch + stored as unmapped + skipped + computed by the target + conflict, so nothing fell through unaccounted (from an old-layout source, the two logs balance against the entries they became, and the journal entries the conversion composed are counted apart, against the plan).
- **No failures:** any `failed` path blocks the flip, and so does any `mismatch`, except at a document the plan named for a No in-profile form construct, or that the author named for this run after a rewrite reproduced its mismatch (§ Copy), which is reported with its difference.
- **The target’s listing, checked** as the source inventory was (§ The preflight): against its binding’s independent count where there is one, for completeness where there is only that.
- **The collections:** each one’s entry count on the target equals the source’s (from an old-layout source, the old logs’ headings, and the journal entries the plan composed), every ID at the source’s number, and each index (stored, or as the target computes it) lists every ID once.

Verification itself re-reads nothing: on a target that normalizes, each document’s content, and each computed index, was already compared at its read-back, and the listing catches a lost write. Anything wrong stops the run before the target’s config and before the flip: report the category, the paths missing or failed, and that re-running resumes.

**Then** the main session writes the target’s `state.md` first (with any What's Next change the move calls for, § Report) and its `.storystormer/config.json` second, the very last write to the target (§ What happens to each item; on Notion the Projects row’s sections), lists the target once more to see they are there (on Notion, that the Projects row now has its sections), and only then **the flip**:

- **A local source:** in the open folder’s config, replace the `storage` block whole with the target’s (`{"kind": "notion", "root": "<row id>"}`, `{"kind": "dropbox", "root": "/StoryStormer/<slug>"}`, with `local_root` only in the hybrid), so the config is the folder’s own `layout` and the target’s `storage` and nothing else. No source document changes; going back is restoring `"storage": {"kind": "local"}`, which returns to the project as it was when it moved, without anything done since. An old-layout source’s config has no `layout` and still holds its mirrored fields: the flip replaces only its `storage` and leaves the rest as it is, so that going back finds the old-layout project exactly as it was (and resolution offers it the upgrade).
- **A connector source with an open folder** whose pointer names this source: rewrite the pointer as exactly `{"storage": {…the target’s block…}}`, the wrapper and nothing else, or, when the target is this folder, the target’s full config has already replaced it.
- **A hybrid source:** the open folder’s config is also the Dropbox project’s config, so the flip changes that one file’s `storage` block; it is the one exception to the untouched source, and restoring the old block undoes it.
- **No config in the open folder** (a local target that was empty, reached from a connector source with no pointer here): the config just written is this folder’s own config. There is no flip.
- **An open folder with no config, and a target that isn’t this folder** (a connector source reached through the locator, moved to another connector): there is no flip, and nothing is written into the folder, since it never pointed at the source. The report says the locator finds the project, as with no open folder.
- **An open folder whose pointer names a different project** (its `storage` block isn’t the source’s: the author reached this source through the locator from another project’s folder): leave the folder alone, write nothing into it, and report that it points at another project and that the locator finds this one.
- **No open folder:** there is nothing to flip. The report gives the new location and says the locator will now list the project on both backends under the same slug until the author deletes the source.
- **Never** a source store’s own config: a Dropbox project’s stored config stays as it was, and a Notion source’s config is computed, so its `storage` is never written.

**After the flip the session moves too.** Replace the binding, root and capabilities line the session remembers (`store.md` § Resolve, “What the session remembers”) with the target’s, by resolving again (with no open folder, bind the target directly, since its root is known, rather than run the locator), so that a decision captured or a document written next lands in the new home and not in the source. The report says the session is now working there.

## The hybrid (local → its own Dropbox)

A local source whose folder syncs through Dropbox, migrated to Dropbox, becomes one store seen two ways: a session with the folder open resolves local, one with no folder resolves Dropbox, and both see the same files. The sync root is the Dropbox folder itself (`~/Dropbox`, `~/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox`), as in `storystormer-init` § Where a new project lives.

- **The folder already is `<Dropbox sync root>/StoryStormer/<slug>`.** No copy at all. The same-slug check finds this folder’s own config, which is not a conflict. Tell that it is this folder’s own by comparing the config the Dropbox binding reads at `/StoryStormer/<slug>/.storystormer/config.json` with the one the local binding reads here: the same (final newline aside) → its own; different → the cloud holds another version (sync behind, or another project under that slug): stop and show the author both. The plan is two writes through the local binding, config last: `state.md` gets the hybrid Storage line (`file-schemas.md` § `state.md`), then the config’s `storage` becomes `{"kind": "dropbox", "root": "/StoryStormer/<slug>", "local_root": "<this folder’s absolute path>"}`. An old-layout folder can’t take that `state.md` write (`store.md` § Resolve step 4): offer the layout upgrade first (§ The layout upgrade, in place, since these are the same files), and plan the hybrid once it is done.
- **The folder syncs through Dropbox but sits elsewhere** (`~/Dropbox/Novels/the-good-dog`). Copy to `/StoryStormer/<slug>`. When the local binding can reach the sync-root path (Claude Code can; a Cowork folder connection that exposes only the project folder can’t), write through the **local binding rooted at `<Dropbox sync root>/StoryStormer/<slug>`** on this machine: the Dropbox binding’s tree is the local tree byte for byte, so these are the files it would hold, written in one native call each instead of delete-create through the connector, and the Dropbox app syncs them up. The same-slug check still runs through the Dropbox binding, which sees the cloud even when this machine’s sync is behind or selective, and the local folder is checked too, through the local binding: it must be absent, or match the approved merge baseline. A folder the cloud doesn’t show but this machine holds (not yet synced) is named and asked about, never overwritten. Verification lists through the local binding; the report says the copy reaches other devices as the Dropbox app syncs it. The new folder gets the full config with `local_root` = its own path and the hybrid Storage line. The old folder’s config is flipped to the same `storage` block, `local_root` included, so opening the old folder resolves to the new synced copy: that `local_root` names the synced copy of the block’s own Dropbox root, the one case where a config outside that folder may carry one (`store.md` § Resolve). When the local binding can’t reach the sync-root path, copy through the Dropbox binding instead, and set `local_root` (in the new config and the flipped one) only if the synced path exists on this machine.
- **A folder outside Dropbox** goes through the Dropbox binding with no `local_root`, unless the author names their Dropbox folder on this machine and it exists (ask; don’t guess), in which case it is the second case.

## Report

- **Where it lives now:** the backend, its root, and the way in (the Dropbox path, the Notion row’s link, the folder), that this session now works there, and how the next session finds it: the open folder resolves there; with no folder, the locator lists it.
- **Dropped from What Exists and computed documents:** the text the plan named, and that the source still holds it.
- **Counts** by category: written, identical (no-op), mismatch (with the paths and the difference), computed by the target, stored as unmapped (with the paths), skipped (with paths and reasons: snapshots on a target without them, non-markdown on Notion, binaries, and the clutter count).
- **Failures** (each path and its error; any blocks the flip, and re-running resumes), **conflicts** and the entries held back with them, **replacements** on a merge, and any entry renumbered.
- **From an old-layout source:** the conversion’s results, as the upgrade reports them (§ The layout upgrade, Report), and that the source is still in the old layout.
- **The source:** where it is, that it is untouched (or, in a hybrid source, that only its config’s `storage` block changed), that `migrate` never deletes it, and that deleting it by hand stops the locator listing two copies. Any snapshots or files that stayed behind are there.
- **Going back:** the `storage` block to restore, and that it returns to the source as it was at the move.
- The binding’s own once-per-run notes: Dropbox’s way to revert; into Notion, the documents normalized and what changed in each, as the plan approved, and visible HTML comments; out of Notion, that text written there or before normalization came back in Notion’s form.

Change the target’s `state.md` → What's Next only if the move changes what the author should do next (it usually doesn’t), and fold that change into the `state.md` write before the config (§ Verify, then flip), so the config stays the last write.

## From an old-layout source

A move whose source is in the old layout (the session resolved it with no `layout`: `store.md` § Resolve step 4) writes the target in layout 2 and leaves the source as it is (`file-schemas.md` § The old layout). The conversion (§ Converting the old layout) runs as part of the copy, on the way in:

- **The collections category** reads the source’s `decisions.md` and `questions.md` and writes each converted entry to the target at its own number; the target’s indexes are built from them (or computed by the target).
- **The main session** composes the journal entries and the slim `state.md` from the source’s `state.md` and writes them in their places in the order: the journal entries before verification, `state.md` and then the config last, as always. The config is `{"layout": 2, "storage": <the target’s block>}`.
- **The plan:** the upgrade’s preflight items (§ The layout upgrade) join the move’s, all but the snapshot and the removal: entries per log, the fields that move and the lines that stay, the composed question titles, the journal entries and what goes into each, what leaves `state.md`, the slug, and the config fields dropped.
- **Nothing is snapshotted or removed:** the source keeps its old files, untouched. The upgrade itself never runs on a move’s source; after the move the source is still in the old layout, and the report says so.

The copy’s own checks then apply to the converted text as written: each converted document passes the conversion’s fidelity check before it is written (§ Converting the old layout), is read back and compared exactly with what was sent (§ Copy), and verification counts entries against the old logs’ headings (§ Verify, then flip). A fidelity failure is a `mismatch` on that entry.

## Converting the old layout

What the layout upgrade (§ The layout upgrade) and a move from an old-layout source (above) both produce from the old documents. It is model work, not a parser: real logs carry more than any template (extra bold fields, run-in bold paragraphs, status prose, header suffixes, a second copy of a question’s fields), and none of it needs to fit the template to be kept.

**Each old entry becomes one entry document**, `decisions/D-###.md` or `questions/Q-###.md` at the entry’s own number, in the shape of `file-schemas.md` § `decisions/` or § `questions/`, with every word of the old entry kept:

- **Frontmatter from the header and the fields.** The header’s parts give `id`, `date` and `type` (a decision) or `id`, `priority` and `category` (a question), each as written (a retired type or category is kept: the schema reads it as its mapped value). The first occurrence of each mapped field gives its key: `Topic` → `topic`, `Source` → `source`, `Integrated into treatment` → `integrated`, `Superseded by` → `superseded_by`, `Title` → `title`, `Date` → `date`, `Status` → `status` (and `resolved_by`, from its first `D-###`, when it says resolved), and in a series `Scope` and `Affects books`. Then `storystormer_project` (the root `state.md`’s `slug`) and `storystormer_kind`. Key order and quoting per `file-schemas.md` (`integrated: "yes"`).
- **A field line leaves the body only when its key holds the whole value as written:** `**Topic**: Core premise`, `**Source**: user-declared (init session)`, `**Integrated into treatment**: n/a — governs future work`, `**Status**: open`. When the value says more than the key can hold (`**Status**: **resolved (D-029)** — Tommy. …`, `**Superseded by**: D-052 — narrowed for Act 3 only`), the key takes what it can (`status: resolved`, `resolved_by: D-029`) and the line stays in the body as written; the frontmatter is authoritative from then on (`file-schemas.md` § Entry collections). A repeat of a mapped label, every other field (`**Resolves**:`, `**Open**:`, `**Note**:`, `**Integrated into structure**:`), run-in bold paragraphs, free paragraphs and sub-lists stay in the body, in the order written.
- **A header suffix** that only restates the status (` ✅ RESOLVED`) is carried by `status`; one with any other words is the body’s first line, as written.
- **The body is in the markdown profile:** `references/markdown-profile.md` § Rewrite table applied, which changes markup only, never a word. The usual case in old logs is a list indented under `**Possible approaches**:`.
- **What it composes:** a question’s `title` when the old entry has none, 3–7 words taken from the question’s own words (`file-schemas.md` § `questions/`), and `status: open` for a question that records no status at all (other wording reads as the closest of the three, its words kept in the body). Nothing else: a `date`, `source` or `integrated` the entry never recorded is left out, never guessed.
- **Not carried:** each log’s own frontmatter (`version`, `count`), its heading and its preamble (the text before the first entry), which have no home in layout 2. The plan names the preamble by its first words and word count; it stays in the source, or in the upgrade’s snapshot.

**Fidelity is checked by a different agent** from the one that converted the entry (another subagent, or the main session), before it is written, as the normalized form is (§ Copy): every word of the old entry is in the new one, in the body or a frontmatter value (a moved field’s label aside); each key holds what its field said; and nothing was added but what the conversion composes (the plan’s title, a `status: open`) and the two `storystormer_*` fields. It compares words, not wording, so markup the profile rewrote doesn’t count. Anything it finds is fixed and checked again before the write.

**The indexes** are built from the converted entries’ frontmatter, one row per entry, sorted by ID numerically (`file-schemas.md` § `decisions/_index.md`, § `questions/_index.md`); a store that computes them gets no write.

**The journal** takes the history `state.md` held, every word of it (`file-schemas.md` § `journal/`):

- **One entry per identifiable past session.** A paragraph or line that opens by naming a session (`2026-08-17 (session 10) — …`) starts that session’s entry, `journal/<its date>-s<its number>.md`, and everything up to the next session’s opening belongs to it; the old Last Session is the latest one. History that names dates but no session numbers is numbered in date order, ending at `session_count`, and the plan shows each number it assigned. History in which no session can be told apart becomes one entry dated `last_session` and numbered `session_count`.
- **The conversion’s own entry,** `journal/<date>-s<N>.md`, where `N` is the old `state.md`’s `session_count` + 1 and the date is the day of the first run, both fixed then: the conversion writes `state.md`, so it is a session (`file-schemas.md` § `state.md`, a new session). Its `skills` names `migrate`, which is how a resumed upgrade finds it; a resume extends that entry (`store.edit`), whatever today’s date, and never creates a second one, so the upgrade stays one session however many runs it takes. `state.md` is written with the same `N` as `session_count`. It records what was converted (counts, the snapshot or version-history route, what was dropped), and holds the history that belongs to no session, each piece under a line saying where it was: a What Exists line’s narrative, a finished What’s Next item, a free paragraph.
- **Frontmatter:** `date`, `session`, `skills` (those the text names, else `[]`), `decisions` and `questions` (the IDs the text names), `source: plugin`, and the two `storystormer_*` fields. Bodies in the profile. Every one is written with `new`, oldest first, the conversion’s own last.

**The slim `state.md`** (the root, and each book’s in a series), per `file-schemas.md` § `state.md`:

- **Frontmatter:** the old keys, less `storage_kind`; on the root, `slug` (§ Source and target’s slug, which on Dropbox is the folder name and on Notion the row’s `Slug`) and `session_count` and `last_session` from the conversion’s own entry; on each book’s `state.md`, `session_count` and `last_session` dropped (sessions are the root’s: `file-schemas.md` § `state.md`), and the plan names the drop; and any value the old config held that a `state.md` lacks (the target, a book’s title or own target), moved into the `state.md` that owns it (`file-schemas.md` § `.storystormer/config.json`). A config value that disagrees with `state.md`’s is dropped, `state.md`’s kept, and the plan names both.
- **Body: the four sections and nothing else.** Summary as written. What Exists one line per artifact, recomputed from the store, with its carried lines kept as written (`file-schemas.md` § `state.md`, update rules); a computed line’s narrative, and any paragraph or retained earlier line in the section, goes to the journal. What’s Next keeps its forward items as written, all of them, even past about five (they are the author’s; the plan says when there are more); a finished item goes to the journal. Last Session is two or three sentences on the conversion, naming its journal entry and the latest working session’s. The H1 is dropped (the title is the frontmatter’s); every other section (`## Previous Session`) goes to the journal.
- **Under about 4 KB,** carried lines not counted, measured before it is written; anything over is history, for the journal. Old projects can be over on forward text alone (a long Summary, What’s Next items that explain themselves at length): then the plan proposes what moves, such as each long item’s explanation after its first sentence, with the item naming the journal entry that holds the rest, and the author decides. The size the author approves is the one the upgrade checks.

**Fan-out.** A log past `references/subagent-pattern.md` § When to dispatch’s threshold (over 5,000 words, or more than a few dozen entries) goes to execution subagents by ID range, decisions and questions apart, dispatched in one message. Each brief carries the store block (binding, root, capabilities line, and the layout: an old-layout project being converted into layout 2), the realizations of the operations it uses with the clauses they trigger, as § Copy’s briefs carry them, this section’s rules for entries, `file-schemas.md` § Entry collections and § `decisions/` or § `questions/` verbatim, the profile’s § Rewrite table and § Kept as written, the slug, its range’s approved titles (the plan’s, never recomposed), and where to read its entries from. It returns a per-entry log (written, identical, kept from an earlier run, conflict, failed) with each entry’s index row and its body’s first words quoted (`subagent-pattern.md` § Quoted-excerpt discipline), and writes no index: the main session builds the indexes from the rows and composes the journal and `state.md` itself, since what counts as a session and as history is judgment about the author’s project.

## The layout upgrade

An old-layout project converted to layout 2 **in place**, on any backend, through the session’s own binding: the store it reads is the store it writes, `storage` doesn’t change, and no other store is touched. In a hybrid session it runs through the local binding, the same files.

**How it starts.** Resolution offers it whenever the project’s config has no `layout` (`store.md` § Resolve step 4), and so does any skill that stops before a layout-dependent write. The author starts it by accepting that offer, by asking (“upgrade this project”), or with `/storystormer:migrate upgrade`. Nothing runs it as a side step of other work. On a layout-2 project there is nothing to upgrade, unless an earlier run left the old logs at the root (Resume), or, on Notion, any Decisions or Questions row of the project still lacks its entry mark (`_entry`: a repaired old state, `references/store/notion.md` § Old-layout projects), in which case the upgrade may be entered again to convert those rows.

**What you produce:** the project in layout 2, per § Converting the old layout: the entry collections, their indexes (stored or computed), the journal, the slim `state.md` files, and the config `{"layout": 2, "storage": <unchanged>}`. On a store with `history: snapshots`, the old documents are kept in `.storystormer/history/<timestamp>-layout-upgrade/` (operation `layout-upgrade`): `decisions.md`, `questions.md`, every `state.md` and the old config, each at its root-relative path, and the two old logs are then removed from the root. On a store with native history (Notion), nothing is snapshotted and nothing is removed: the old logs were computed from the rows, which are converted in place, each keeping its prior versions in the backend’s own history (`references/store/notion.md` § Old-layout projects). The plan says so, and that the two logs as rendered wholes have no history of their own there; no extra snapshot is written, since the binding never stores `.storystormer/history/**` and each converted row’s own history is the record.

**The preflight is the plan.** Read both logs, every `state.md` and the config; list `decisions/`, `questions/`, `journal/` and `.storystormer/history/` for an earlier run’s remains. Then show, writing nothing:

- **Entries per log,** counted from the `## D-###` and `## Q-###` headings, with each log’s ID range and any gap (kept as it is). A number that heads two entries stops the plan for the author’s call, since one path can’t hold both.
- **What moves and what stays:** each mapped field with how many entries move it into the frontmatter and how many keep their line because it says more (`Status`: 19 move, 22 stay because they carry their resolution); entries left without `source` or `integrated`; the markup the profile rewrites, by construct and count; each log’s preamble, not carried.
- **The question titles it will compose,** each with its ID: the only author-facing text the upgrade writes, so the author can change any before the go.
- **The journal:** each entry it will create, by path, with the first words of its history and its word count; each piece of unattributed history going to the upgrade’s own entry, by where it was and its word count; and how session numbers were assigned, when the history gave none.
- **What leaves `state.md`:** the H1 and `storage_kind`, dropped, by their text; in a series, each book’s `session_count` and `last_session`, dropped; every other section by name and word count, to the journal; each finished What’s Next item; the slim file’s size against the budget, and when it is over, what the plan proposes to move.
- **The slug** it will set, and what it came from.
- **The new config,** exactly as it will be written, and each mirrored field it drops, with any value moved into a `state.md` and any disagreement.
- **The old files:** the snapshot path, and that the two logs leave the root once the collections verify; or, on native history, the version-history note above.
- **Call volume,** roughly: the reads, one write per entry, journal entry and index (Notion batches its creates, per its binding), the snapshot, and verification’s reads, one per entry.

Then wait for the go, which covers every write and the removal (`store.md` § Confirmation posture).

**Order.** A failure at any step stops the run with nothing removed; re-running resumes.

1. **Snapshot** the old documents as one set (`store.md` § Local-mode clauses, snapshot), before anything else is written. A resume first completes the snapshot: any of the set still at the root in its old form and not yet in the run’s folder goes in, before anything else. When several `*-layout-upgrade/` folders exist, the latest is the run’s.
2. **Entries**, decisions before questions, each written at its own number with a plain `write` once the listing has shown its path absent. Never `new`: on a file store a `new` that meets an existing path takes the next number, and a carried-over entry’s number is what chapters cite.
3. **Indexes**, where the store keeps them.
4. **Journal entries**, with `new`.
5. **Verify the collections:** every entry passed its fidelity check; each collection’s entry count equals its old log’s headings; every ID is there at its own number; every entry reads back through the store and matches what was written (the final newline aside); each index, stored or computed, lists every ID once; every journal entry the plan named exists.
6. **`state.md`** (each book’s, then the root’s), checked against the approved size before it is written.
7. **The config, `layout: 2`, the last write.** From here every skill writes again.
8. **Remove** `decisions.md` and `questions.md` from the root (`store.delete`), each only after a read shows it identical to its snapshot copy. A store that can’t delete names the paths for the author to remove (its binding’s `delete`), and the report names them as safe to remove (the snapshot holds identical copies) and says layout 2 never reads them.

On Notion the binding orders the steps for its rows (`references/store/notion.md` § Old-layout projects): its schema upgrade first, each row converted in place by its entry’s `write`, then the journal, then step 5 on the entries, which a converted row serves at its path in any layout (counts, read-back, and no Decisions or Questions row of the project left without `_entry`), then `state.md`, and `Layout` = 2 last, only once that verification has passed; the indexes, computed, are checked after it. No step 1 or 8. A failure before `Layout` leaves the project in the old layout with its converted rows as entries, and running the upgrade again finishes it.

**Resume.** Running it again on a project an earlier run left part-way shows the plan with what is done and does only the rest:

- **An entry already at its path** is kept when it keeps every word of its old entry with its fields right, checked against the old entry rather than against a new conversion (two conversions of one entry may differ harmlessly). One that doesn’t (half-written, edited since) is shown with both texts, and the go repairs it as an entry: an ordinary `write` at its path of the entry composed from the old text, never a second conversion (on Notion a converted row holds `_entry`, and the conversion refuses it).
- **The count check’s baseline** on a resumed run is the old logs’ headings as the first run read them: on a file store the snapshot’s logs; on Notion, where a converted row leaves the rendered log, the headings of the log as it renders now plus the rows already converted.
- **A journal entry** whose `new` write finds its path taken is skipped when it holds the text the plan gives it, else shown with both; it never takes the next session number, as a new session’s entry would. The upgrade’s own entry is found by its `skills` and extended (the conversion’s own entry, above).
- **The earlier snapshot folder** (the latest `*-layout-upgrade/`) is the old text for anything the root no longer holds in its old form (the old `state.md`, once step 6 ran, and with it the `session_count` the upgrade’s number comes from), and a resume never snapshots over it.
- **A layout-2 config with the old logs still at the root** means only step 8 is left: offer it, after the same identity check.

**Report:** counts per collection (written, kept from an earlier run, rewritten), the journal entries created, the titles composed, what was dropped (the H1, `storage_kind`, the config’s mirrored fields, each log’s preamble), where the old text is (the snapshot folder, or the version-history route on Notion), the old logs removed, or left at the root for the author, named as safe to remove since the snapshot holds identical copies, and that the project is now in layout 2.

## What this skill does not do

- **Delete or edit a move’s source**, or offer to. The single write outside the target is the flip. (The layout upgrade has no source apart from its own store: it converts that store in place and removes the two old logs only after they are snapshotted and the collections verify.)
- **Sync two stores.** After the flip the target is the project; the hybrid is one store seen two ways, not two stores kept in step.
- **Convert files.** A binary or non-markdown file the target can’t hold stays where it is and is named.
- **Improve content on the way.** Documents cross as they are, apart from the `storage` fields, the What Exists recount, into a target that normalizes the markup normalization the plan approved, and from an old-layout source the conversion. The conversion moves words, never rewrites them: the composed question titles are the only text it writes.
